Design notes & open questions

Why the mocks look the way they do, what they assume, and what the team still needs to decide. [TBD] marks values that aren't settled.
PRD review: top 5 and 22 blockers

Design decisions

  1. One component, two densities. The full module (Dashboard) and the compact module (Agent Home) share data, states and events. A placement test then changes only where the list sits and how dense it is.
  2. The reason comes first. The plain-language reason is the main line of each item. The tier is a small High/Medium label with a bar icon. We never show raw scores, which agents can't act on.
  3. Plain-language signal chips. "Likely to sell" and "Browsing more homes" instead of BFY or IWA. Each chip has an icon and text, so meaning never depends on colour. A client with both signals shows both chips.
  4. Worked items stay in place until refresh. Contacted items turn grey with a "Contacted" label plus how and when. Dismissed items are struck through with a "Dismissed" label. Keeping their rank position stops the list from jumping under the cursor. Refresh clears them and fills open slots.
  5. Dismiss takes one click, and the reason is optional. The reason chips appear after the strikethrough, show the suppression window as you pick, and say plainly that dismissing doesn't reassign the client. "Not my client" has a [TBD] warning until reassignment rules are decided.
  6. Actions open inside the row. In-app call bar, tap-to-call panel for agents without in-app calling, and text and email composers. Only one action panel is open at a time, so the list stays readable. Ending a call or sending a message marks the client as contacted and logs it automatically.
  7. "Mark as called" is on every open item, even for agents with in-app calling, because many still dial from their own phone. It shows an undo toast and the dedupe note.
  8. Reason source is visible but secondary. The expanded view says whether an AI wrote the reason or it came from a template, and when. Fallback or missing reasons are labelled on the row itself.
  9. Flagging a reason takes one click and doesn't remove the client. An optional "What's wrong?" follow-up collects detail without forcing it.
  10. Open tasks show on the item. The copy says contacting from the list completes the task too (the PRD's mitigation for task overlap).
  11. Short and empty lists explain themselves. "That's everyone with a fresh signal today" plus the exclusion rules, instead of padding the list with weak suggestions.
  12. Accessibility. Real buttons whose names include the visible label. Visible focus ring. aria-expanded on expanders. Escape closes panels. Toasts in a polite live region. Greyed text stays at AA contrast (#6C6C6C on #FAFAFA). The slide-in respects reduced motion.

Fair Housing-safe reason copy

  • Reasons and summaries only use property and behaviour signals: time in home, estimated value or equity change, nearby sales and listings, listings viewed, and saved searches.
  • They never mention or imply demographics, family status, age, life stage, health, religion or national origin. No "growing family", "empty nesters" or "retiring". Reasons avoid gendered pronouns.
  • IWA reasons use one fixed template: "Viewed N for-sale listings in the past 7 days, X times the busiest week in the prior month." An optional saved-search clause can be added.

Assumptions

  • The CRM Dashboard sits under Contacts in the left nav, and Agent Home is the existing Home.
  • The sample agent is Marcus Lee. It's 11:48 a.m. local time, and the pool was built at 5:42 a.m.
  • Suppression windows (14, 30 and 90 days) and the dedupe window are placeholders.
  • After a refresh, open items keep their order and new items join at the bottom. The progress count covers the current list, and worked items that refresh cleared count as "earlier today".
  • Texts go from the agent's Compass number. Emails go from the connected mailbox.
  • Working an item on one surface updates it everywhere (one source of truth).

PRD review questions (Senior PM)

From the Senior PM's review of the Oct 20, 2026 draft. Review only: the PRD itself hasn't been edited. "Design Q" links point to the questions above. Amber notes mark places where the mocks differ from the PM's proposed default.

Overall assessment

The problem framing is strong. The history section (low adoption of the standalone LTS page, cap removal, reassignment on dismiss) gives the most useful context in the doc. The event table and attribution windows are more developed than in most P1 PRDs. The main gaps are in the core mechanics that design and engineering need to build against. There's no rule for how BFY and IWA items are merged and ranked. Dismiss isn't clearly separated from the existing reassignment path, which is the biggest risk to agent trust. Shared-contact behaviour contradicts the "ranked for the individual agent" claim. The success metrics have no primary metric, no baselines and no targets, so the GA exit criterion ("primary metric meets target (TBD)") can't be tested yet. Finally, the evidence (FUB teams asking for "smart lists" and "call lists") only partly supports this feature. The PRD should say plainly which parts are validated and which are a bet.
